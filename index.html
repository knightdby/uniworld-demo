UniWorld: A Universal Controllable World Model for Autonomous Driving

Abstract

We present UniWorld, a universal framework for scene reasoning and controllable scene generation in autonomous driving, formulated with three key components. First, we introduce UniWorld-R, the first large-scale Scene Reasoning Model, pretrained on a newly established traffic scene reasoning benchmark. It achieves a paradigm shift from single-vehicle prediction to multi-agent interaction reasoning, which allows joint modeling of complex traffic behaviors and language instruction controllable traffic-flow manipulation. Second, we design UniWorld-A, a action policy model that reconstructs and predicts traffic flows in a closed-loop manner. It captures real-world dynamics while supporting language-conditioned control of agent trajectories. Third, we propose UniWorld-G, a video generation model that translates reconstructed traffic flows into high-fidelity surround-view videos with consistent annotations, including 3D bounding boxes, BEV map, and semantic occupancy prediction. In addition, we release the first large-scale traffic scene reasoning benchmark, SceneVerse-480k-R1, which provides a foundation for training multi-agent interaction reasoning. These components collectively establish an end-to-end language-to-data pipeline for controllable scene generation, which supports the synthesis of large-scale, diverse autonomous driving datasets. Extensive experiments demonstrate that UniWorld substantially improves scene reasoning, traffic flow reconstruction, scene generation, and downstream tasks, which establish a new paradigm for controllable video generation method.
